**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3390/foods12224175  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10670562/  
**Open access:** Yes (MDPI Foods)

**Crop:** *Solanum tuberosum* L. (6 potato clones: 3 purple [P1=CIP302288.14, P2=CIP302288.35, P3=CIP302288.39], 1 yellow [Y=CIP302286.26], 2 red [R1=CIP302284.17, R2=CIP302286.13])

**Sites:**

| Site | Location | Altitude (m) | Approx. lat/lon |
|------|----------|-------------|-----------------|

| High | Lhasa City, Tibet AR, China | 3,600 | ~29.7°N, 91.1°E |

**Anthocyanin concentrations (mg/g FW, total):**

| Clone | High (3,600 m) | Mid (1,600 m) | Low (800 m) |
|-------|---------------|--------------|------------|
| P1 (purple) | 4.173 | 1.858 | 2.197 |
| P2 (purple) | 0.349 | 0.132 | 0.345 |

### PAPER 2

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2024.1501889  
**PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11576316/  
**Open access:** Yes (Frontiers)

**Crop:** *Rubus glaucus* Benth (Andean blackberry / "mora de Castilla")

**Sites:**

| Site | Location | Altitude (m) | Coordinates |
|------|----------|-------------|-------------|

| Ripeness stage | 3,200 m | 2,360 m |
|----------------|---------|---------|
| Stage 1 (<50% pigmented) | 0.13 ± 0.01 | 0.08 ± 0.01 |
| Stage 2 (75% pigmented) | 1.22 ± 0.09 | 0.81 ± 0.04 |
| Stage 3 (fully ripe) | 5.25 ± 0.06 | 3.94 ± 0.06 |

Higher altitude site consistently ~25–35% higher anthocyanin at matching ripeness stage.

### PAPER 3
**Spinardi, A., Cola, G., Gardana, C.S., & Mignani, I. (2019).** Variation of anthocyanin content and profile throughout fruit development and ripening of highbush blueberry cultivars grown at two different altitudes. *Frontiers in Plant Science*, 10, 1045.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2019.1045  
**Open access:** Yes (Frontiers)

**Crop:** *Vaccinium corymbosum* L. (highbush blueberry), cultivars 'Duke' and 'Brigitta'

**Sites:**

| Site | Location | Altitude (m) | Coordinates |
|------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Postalesio | Valtellina, Northern Italy | 450 | 46.175°N, 9.776°E |
| Gaggio di Berbenno | Valtellina, Northern Italy | 650 | 46.168°N, 9.746°E |

**Anthocyanin concentrations:** Reported as µg/g FW, detected as monoarabinosides, monogalactosides, and monoglucosides of delphinidin, cyanidin, petunidin, peonidin, malvidin, and acetyled petunidin-galactoside. Quantitative differences: 'Duke' berries showed 432% increase during ripening at lower site vs 1,192% increase at higher site. At final ripeness (class IV), no statistically significant difference in total anthocyanins between the two altitude sites was detected in ripe fruit. [NOTE: Specific mg/g values for ripe fruit at each site require full paper access — the altitude difference here (200 m) is modest and concentration difference at harvest was not significant.]

**Method:** ACQUITY UHPLC system with photodiode array detector and Exactive high-resolution mass spectrometer (ESI); Kinetex C18 column (150×4.6 mm, 2.6 µm).

**Notes:** Important methodological caveat — the altitude difference is only 200 m and mature fruit concentrations did not differ significantly. Rate of accumulation during ripening differed. This paper is useful for understanding ripening kinetics but may not produce strong altitude signal for RSU. Include with caveat.

### PAPER 5
**Zoratti, L., Jaakola, L., Häggman, H., & Giongo, L. (2015).** Anthocyanin profile in berries of wild and cultivated Vaccinium spp. along altitudinal gradients in the Alps. *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry*, 63(39), 8641–8650.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jafc.5b02833  
**Open access:** No (ACS, paywalled)

**Crop:** *Vaccinium myrtillus* L. (wild bilberry) + *Vaccinium corymbosum* L. cv. 'Brigitta Blue' (cultivated highbush blueberry)

**Sites:** Northern Italian Alps, across an altitudinal gradient of approximately 650 m (specific site altitudes and coordinates not accessible from abstract — require full paper access)

**Anthocyanin concentrations:** Quantitative total bilberry values in literature reported as 236–656 mg/100g FW in various European studies; this paper shows increasing accumulation with altitude over ~650 m gradient. Specific per-site mg values are in the full paper (paywalled).

**Key finding:** Significant increases in delphinidin and malvidin glycosides with altitude; cyanidin and peonidin glycosides unaffected by altitude.

**Individual anthocyanins:** Delphinidin, cyanidin, petunidin, peonidin, malvidin glycosides (standard Vaccinium myrtillus 15-anthocyanin profile)

**Method:** HPLC (standard reversed-phase with DAD detection at 520 nm)

**Notes:** This is the canonical Alps bilberry altitude paper and is widely cited. Full data tables require institutional access. If accessible, this would give the strongest Vaccinium altitude gradient data for European Alps RSUs. Flag for library access.

### PAPER 7
**Senica, M., Stampar, F., Veberic, R., & Mikulic-Petkovsek, M. (2017).** The higher the better? Differences in phenolics and cyanogenic glycosides in *Sambucus nigra* leaves, flowers and berries from different altitudes. *Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture*, 97(8), 2623–2632.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/jsfa.8085  
**Open access:** No (Wiley, paywalled)

**Crop:** *Sambucus nigra* L. (black elderberry)

**Sites:** 2 locations (Slovenia, most likely — University of Ljubljana, Biotechnical Faculty affiliation), each sampled at multiple altitudes (foothills vs hilltop). Exact altitudes and coordinates not accessible from abstract.

**Anthocyanin concentrations (from abstract snippet):**
- Location 2, foothills: **3,343 µg/g FW** (= 334.3 mg/100g FW)
- Location 2, hilltop: **7,729 µg/g FW** (= 772.9 mg/100g FW)

Higher elevation = ~2.3× higher anthocyanin in elderberry fruit.

**Key finding:** Cyanogenic glycosides and anthocyanins both affected by altitude, but in opposite biological roles. Effect of latitude and longitude on anthocyanin content was NOT significant — altitude was the significant geographic driver.

**Individual anthocyanins:** Elderberry characteristic profile: cyanidin-3-sambubioside and cyanidin-3-glucoside (major); cyanidin-3-sambubioside-5-glucoside and cyanidin-3,5-diglucoside (minor). HPLC method confirmed.

**Method:** HPLC (details in full paper — reversed phase, DAD)

**Notes:** Possibly the only primary research paper with direct elderberry altitude data and specific µg/g values from the abstract itself. 3,343 vs 7,729 µg/g is a large and meaningful difference. Location is almost certainly Slovenia (based on author affiliations at UL Ljubljana). Altitude values not in abstract — require full paper. High priority for library access.

### PAPER 8
**Guerrero-Chavez, G., Scampicchio, M., & Andreotti, C. (2015).** Influence of the site altitude on strawberry phenolic composition and quality. *Scientia Horticulturae*, 192, 21–28.  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scienta.2015.05.017  
**Open access:** No (ScienceDirect, paywalled)

**Crop:** *Fragaria × ananassa* Duch. cv. 'Elsanta'

**Sites:**
- Martello Valley, Province of Bolzano, northern Italy (46°33′N, 10°47′E)
- 4 altitude sites: **900, 1,100, 1,200, and 1,500 m** a.s.l.

**Anthocyanin concentrations:** Specific mg/100g values not in abstract, but the paper reports a **significant negative correlation between altitude and anthocyanin concentration** (R = −0.55; p ≤ 0.01), and a 36% reduction in antioxidant potential from 900 m to 1,500 m. Anthocyanin synthesis decreased with higher altitude.

NOTE: This is the **opposite direction** from most other crops — strawberry anthocyanins decrease with altitude in this study. This may relate to temperature depression at altitude reducing enzymatic activity, or lower UV reaching fruit beneath the canopy. This is an important contrasting data point.

**Method:** HPLC (details in full paper); anthocyanin quantification by HPLC/DAD; electrochemical method also used for antioxidant potential.

**Notes:** 4-site altitude gradient with exact location specified. Negative altitude-anthocyanin relationship is scientifically interesting and relevant for TerraMetabolica RSU models — not all metabolites respond uniformly to altitude. Specific mg/100g values need full paper access.

## OPEN RESEARCH GAPS

1. **No berry-tissue (not wine) Vitis vinifera altitude primary paper with HPLC concentrations was found open-access.** The Berli 2008 paper exists but uses capillary electrophoresis. A verified berry-skin paper with 2+ altitude sites and mg/g values is still needed. Try: Fanzone et al. (2011) *Food Chem.* (DOI: 10.1016/j.foodchem.2011.01.009) for Malbec berry skin phenolics.

2. **Strawberry negative altitude correlation (Guerrero-Chavez 2015)** is the only Fragaria altitude paper found. It is paywalled. Consider it a priority for library access.

3. **Sambucus nigra altitude data (Senica 2017)** shows dramatic difference (3,343 vs 7,729 µg/g) but exact site altitudes not in abstract. Priority library request.

4. **Red cabbage (Brassica oleracea):** No altitude gradient primary paper found beyond Shukla 2024 (already in database). This crop gap is real.

5. **Aronia / black currant altitude studies:** None found with specific multi-site altitude data. This gap is real.
