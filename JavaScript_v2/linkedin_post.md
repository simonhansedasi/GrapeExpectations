Hawaiian specialty coffee is about to have a very hard decade, at least in the region everyone has actually heard of. Kona's thermal window is contracting upslope under projected warming, and by the 2035 horizon there will not be much mountain left for it to retreat into.

Ka'u, on the other side of the Big Island, is having the opposite decade. Same warming, same island, and its suitable zone is expanding into land it never covered before.

I mapped 385 hexagonal cells across both districts at 500m resolution: elevation, slope, satellite vegetation, soil, three decades of climate data. Rather than assume a thermal optimum, I measured each district's directly. Kona sits at 18.4°C with a narrow tolerance; Ka'u sits at 19.9°C with a wider one. The warming projected for both districts is identical; the tolerance each one carries into it is not, and that gap is what decides which one runs out of room.

Climate-only suitability models miss this because they treat temperature as the whole story; terrain decides how much room a region has before it runs out of mountain.

## Version 2 — built around Figure 4 (attach Fig4.jpg)

By 2035, sixty-four percent of Kona's farmable land is projected to fall out of thermal range for coffee. By 2045, that number climbs to seventy percent.

This map is why. Blue cells are the only ground left where a Kona-viable thermal signal actually overlaps farmable terrain, mostly a fringe between 700 and 900 meters, and it keeps shrinking as warming continues. Red cells tell the opposite story on the other side of the island: land where Ka'u's wider thermal tolerance is opening up farmable ground it never had before. Grey is the trap, land that looks suitable if you only check the temperature and is not, because slope, soil, or elevation rule it out anyway.

That grey zone is the whole point of the paper. Run a climate-only suitability model on Ka'u and it says the entire farmable island gains ground: all 40,099 cells. Add the terrain constraint back in and the real number is 2,062 cells, about one-twentieth of what the climate-only version promised. Kona runs the opposite direction: a thermal refuge that climate-only models find on paper, but that terrain does not make available.
